# World and Lore

## The Fading
A gray tide that advances across the land, dissolving whatever it touches. It follows three rules:
1. **The Fading takes.** It consumes people, villages, and forests regardless of whether anyone remembers them.
2. **The Fading erodes memory.** Once something is consumed, the memories of it begin to erode. Survivors forget faces first, then names, then that the thing ever existed.
3. **Restoration requires two things:** a memory of what was lost (the blueprint) and the power of a Crystal Shard (the material). Without the memory, a Shard has nothing to rebuild. Without a Shard, the memory is only grief.

## The Lumari and the Memory Vaults
- The Lumari were an ancient civilization that saw the first Fading coming and knew they could not stop it. *(This is what the story first presents. See True History for what actually happened.)*
- They built the Memory Vaults as archives: complete records of their world and of themselves, sealed underground where the tide could not reach.
- Each Vault grows around a **Crystal of Remembrance**, which stores memory the way a battery stores charge.
- The Lumari planned to wait out the Fading and be restored afterward. That restoration never happened. **Why it never happened is the central mystery of the story.** See True History below.
- Only fragmentary Echoes of the Lumari remain, deep in the oldest Vaults.

## Why Adventurers Enter
- The Fading has returned, and Shards are the only known way to push it back and restore what was lost.
- Kingdoms, guilds, and desperate individuals send parties into the Vaults.
- Individual heroes have personal motives: glory, saving a fading hometown, or recovering a loved one taken by the Fading before their own memory of that person erodes completely. This last motive gives heroes a personal ticking clock.

## Memories and Legends
- Heroes grow by absorbing memories inside the Vaults. Each memory shifts the hero's alignment according to how they acted to earn it.
- A hero who reaches a Legendary class is channeling a legend from the past, such as an ancient Lumari knight whose memory they have fully absorbed.
- The first time a player reaches a given Legendary class, that legend is remembered into Lanternrest as a lore NPC who brings their story with them. *(Tentative.)*

## Echoes (Async PvP in the Lore)
- The Vaults are archives, so they record every party that enters. Each party leaves an **Echo** that relives its memories of the descent.
- **The fallen:** Heroes who die in a Vault never leave. They remain trapped as their Echo, reliving their last memories.
- **The victorious:** Heroes who complete a Vault return home, but their Echo remains below.
- Rival parties met in the Vaults are Echoes of real players' parties that descended before you. An Echo fights the way its party did, which is why it behaves like a snapshot of that party.
- Every run, won or lost, leaves a permanent mark on the world.

## The Vault's Judgment
- A Vault yields its Shard to only one party per descent: the party whose memory it judges most worth keeping.
- Riddles, chance events, and moral choices are the Vault evaluating each party.

## Lanternrest
- Lanternrest is the last town at the edge of the Fading and serves as the player's hub. It begins mostly gray and half-erased.
- The central lantern's light is tethered to every party that leaves the town. When a party falls in a Vault, the lantern pulls home fragments of what they experienced (Glimmers and lore), but the fallen heroes stay behind as Echoes. This is why losing a run still advances the world.

## True History (Hidden From Players)
*This is the truth behind the setting, revealed gradually through the story.*

1. **The draw:** The Lumari powered their civilization by drawing memory out of the world into their crystals. The world grew thin.
2. **The first Fading:** The thinning world began to unravel. The Lumari had caused it and could not stop it.
3. **The sealing:** The Lumari used the same crystals to build the Vaults and recorded themselves into them as memory. Their bodies were consumed. **The Lumari are stored inside the Vaults as records**, and the fragmentary Echoes in the oldest Vaults are pieces of them.
4. **The Keeper:** One Lumari stayed outside with a crystal lantern, tasked with restoring the others once the tide passed. Non-Lumari survivors took refuge in its light, and that refuge became **Lanternrest**.
5. **The recovery:** With the Lumari sealed away, nothing was drawing memory. The world slowly healed, the tide receded, and people repopulated the land.
6. **The return:** The Fading came back *(cause TBD)*.
7. **The present:** The tide is advancing again, and adventurers descend into the Vaults for Shards.

### The Lantern and the Keeper
- The lantern's light is fueled by memory, and the only memory available to it was the Keeper's own.
- Every year the lantern held back the Fading, it burned more of what the Keeper knew, until he forgot his name, his people, and what he was waiting to do.
- The lantern gathers the fragments fallen parties carry as fuel, which is why it pulls them home.
- A dimming lantern can no longer hold everything, which is why Lanternrest is half-erased.
- *Possible hook:* the Keeper is the town's strange, forgetful old lamplighter, and nobody recognizes him.

### Why the Lumari Were Never Restored
- The Keeper held the blueprint, his memory of the Lumari, and spent it to keep the last town alive. He chose the living over the dead, one memory at a time.
- *Room for ambiguity:* he may have done this deliberately, because he remembered what the Lumari had done.

### Why Shards Restore
- The Fading began when the Lumari drew memory out of the world. A Shard does the opposite: it releases stored memory back into the world, which is why it can restore what was lost.

## In-World Beliefs
Knowledge of the Lumari is sparse because of the Fading's second rule: the Lumari were consumed, so the world has been forgetting them ever since. What survives does so only in fragments: ruins, the Vaults themselves, half-remembered songs, and children's rhymes.

**Common folk**
- The Lumari were "the bright people" who lived long ago and built the Vaults to protect their treasures.
- The Fading is like weather, a tide that comes and goes with the ages.
- Shards are the Lumari's gift, left behind for whoever is brave enough to claim them.

**Clergy**
- The Fading is a judgment on the world for its pride or for forgetting the old ways.
- Opinions on descending into the Vaults are split between pilgrimage and sacrilege.

**Adventurers**
- The Vaults are alive and judge whoever enters, a superstition that happens to be true.
- People who die in the Vaults never come back. Rival parties met below are believed to be ghosts or curses.
- Practical rumors, such as which floors are deadliest and which rival parties to fear.

**Lanternrest residents**
- The lantern has always burned, lit by a founding saint whose name nobody can recall.

### What Nobody Knows (Story Reveals)
- That the Lumari caused the Fading.
- That the Lumari are stored inside the Vaults.
- That the lantern burns memory, and that the Keeper exists.
- That Echoes are the trapped fallen and the lingering records of every party that entered.
- Why the Fading returned.

### Delivery Hook: Rewritten Codex Entries
Codex entries start out recording the in-world belief (e.g. "The Lumari built the Vaults to guard their treasures") and are **rewritten** as true memories are restored. Restoring the past literally corrects the record.

## Open Questions
- **Why the Fading returned.** Candidates:
  - The Vault crystals are still drawing memory slowly, and after centuries the world has grown thin again.
  - The world never fully healed, and the first Fading's wound has reopened.
- The identity and story of each legend tied to a Legendary class.
- Why Relics bind to the hero who equips them.
- How alignment fits the lore (e.g. as how the Vault remembers each hero).
- Whether the story's ending involves restoring the Lumari, and whether the player becomes the new Keeper.
